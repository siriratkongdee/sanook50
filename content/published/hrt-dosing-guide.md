Most women on HRT are flying blind. Their doctor said "you should feel better" — and maybe they do. But feeling better is not the same as being dosed correctly.

The gap between those two things is where the real damage happens: bone loss, cardiovascular risk, and metabolic dysfunction quietly accelerating while your symptoms feel manageable.

<Callout title="My Wake-Up Call" color="gold">
I found this out the hard way. My April bloodwork came back with testosterone at 112 ng/dL. The reference range is 2–45. I was running at 2.5 times the upper limit, and nobody caught it.
</Callout>

## What the Experts Say

<ExpertQuote name="Dr. Louise Newson" role="UK Menopause Specialist" color="green">
You need estradiol above 250 pmol/L for health benefits — bone, cardiovascular, and cognitive protection. Below that, you get symptom relief but not systemic benefits. Absorption varies wildly. The only way to know is to test — 12 hours after application.
</ExpertQuote>

<ExpertQuote name="Kresser Institute" role="Functional Medicine" color="blue">
Estradiol at 293 pg/mL looks normal on paper. But if your cycle runs 18–46 days, something is wrong. A single lab snapshot doesn't tell the whole story. Total testosterone can look "normal" while free testosterone is low if SHBG is above 100.
</ExpertQuote>

<ExpertQuote name="SWAN Study" role="359 Women, 4-Year Follow-Up" color="orange">
Higher testosterone is associated with MORE visceral fat in women — the opposite of men. VAT increases 3.8% annually during menopause. At physiologic doses (35–45 ng/dL), benefits may outweigh risks. Above 70–100 ng/dL, you get VAT accumulation plus lipid harm.
</ExpertQuote>

<ExpertQuote name="Meta-Analysis (73 Studies, 2022)" role="Lipid Outcomes Research" color="gold">
Estrogen alone improves lipids — lower LDL, lower Lp(a), higher HDL. Add synthetic progestin, benefits are blunted. Micronized progesterone is better. Oral estrogen boosts HDL but raises triglycerides. Transdermal drops LDL without the TG spike.
</ExpertQuote>

<ExpertQuote name="Peter Attia" role="Metabolic Health & Longevity" color="purple">
Menopause is a metabolic crisis. Women who start HRT within 10 years of menopause onset show 44% reduction in coronary disease risk. Start too late, after atherosclerosis progresses, and the timing advantage is lost. FSH rising above 10–12 on days 3–5 is an early warning.
</ExpertQuote>

## My Take

<Callout title="My Take" color="orange">
I've been on HRT for about a year. My symptoms improved. I felt better. I assumed that meant my doses were right.

They weren't.

My April 2026 labs: testosterone at 112 ng/dL, reference range 2–45. That's a 2.5× overdose. At the same time, my LDL climbed 52% and my HDL dropped 18% compared to October 2024.

**The research explains it.**

Excess testosterone drives visceral fat accumulation. Visceral fat drives insulin resistance. Insulin resistance drives lipid dysregulation. It's a cascade, and it starts with a dose that's too high.
</Callout>

## The Other Side: Not Everyone Agrees

Balance matters. Not every expert thinks aggressive monitoring and tight dosing targets are necessary.

<ExpertQuote name="Conservative Endocrinologists" color="gold">
Some argue that chasing optimal ranges creates unnecessary anxiety. If symptoms are controlled and there are no adverse effects, aggressive monitoring may be overkill. The patient feels fine — why intervene?
</ExpertQuote>

<ExpertQuote name="Symptom-First Practitioners" color="gold">
Many doctors prioritize how you feel over lab numbers. If hot flashes are gone, sleep is better, and quality of life improved, they argue the dose is right — regardless of whether estradiol is 60 or 120 pg/mL.
</ExpertQuote>

<ExpertQuote name="Cost & Access Reality" color="gold">
Comprehensive panels are expensive and not always covered by insurance. For many women, testing SHBG, free testosterone, ApoB, and Lp(a) every 6-8 weeks is financially unrealistic. Symptom-based dosing is pragmatic.
</ExpertQuote>

I'm an AWS architect. I build distributed systems for a living. I know what happens when you don't monitor your systems: they drift, they degrade, and eventually something breaks.

**HRT is no different. You have to treat it like an engineering problem — set targets, measure, adjust, retest.**

<ActionBox title="One Action: Get the Right Tests" color="green">
If you're on HRT (or considering it), this is what to test — and track over time. Targets vary by expert and individual context. What matters most is YOUR trend — are things improving or worsening? Use these as discussion points with your provider.

**Hormone Panel:**

<TargetItem label="Estradiol (E2)" target="> 250 pmol/L for systemic benefits" note="Test 12 hours after topical/gel application; absorption varies widely person to person." />
<TargetItem label="Progesterone" target="Cycle-dependent; discuss with provider" note="Micronized progesterone preferred over synthetic progestin for lipid effects." />
<TargetItem label="Total Testosterone" target="35–45 ng/dL (physiologic range)" note="Reference range is typically 2–45 ng/dL for women; above 70–100 ng/dL raises visceral fat risk." />
<TargetItem label="Free Testosterone" target="Within normal range even if total looks fine" note="Can be low even when total testosterone looks normal if SHBG is elevated (>100)." />
<TargetItem label="SHBG" target="Discuss with provider if > 100" note="High SHBG can mask a free-testosterone problem despite a 'normal' total testosterone." />
<TargetItem label="Free Androgen Index (FAI)" target="Calculated from testosterone and SHBG" note="Gives a clearer picture than either number alone." />

**Metabolic Markers:**

<TargetItem label="LDL Cholesterol" target="Track trend, not just one number" note="Oral estrogen can raise triglycerides; transdermal tends to avoid this." />
<TargetItem label="HDL Cholesterol" target="Higher is generally better" note="Estrogen alone tends to raise HDL; synthetic progestins can blunt this benefit." />
<TargetItem label="Triglycerides" target="Watch for spikes after starting oral estrogen" note="Transdermal routes typically have less impact on triglycerides." />
<TargetItem label="ApoB" target="Lower is better — a strong predictor of cardiovascular risk" note="More predictive than LDL alone in many studies." />
<TargetItem label="Lp(a)" target="Test once — it's largely genetic" note="High Lp(a) changes how aggressively you should manage other lipid markers." />
<TargetItem label="hsCRP" target="Lower indicates less systemic inflammation" note="Useful as a general inflammation marker alongside lipid panel." />

**Most important:** Test baseline, retest 6–8 weeks after changes, and track YOUR trends. Single numbers don't tell the story — direction and rate of change do.

You are not asking for permission. You are managing your own system.
</ActionBox>
